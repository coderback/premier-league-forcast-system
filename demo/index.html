Premier League forecasting system

A forecaster built to fail honestly

A Dixon-Coles match model with a season simulator, wrapped in a walk-forward acceptance harness whose job is to reject ideas. Explore its season-table projections against what happened, how far it sits from the bookmakers' closing line, and the long list of ideas it tested and closed.

Saved results, read-only. Every figure comes from the repo's saved output files and CLOSED-LINES.md. The "actual" tables are computed from the cached football-data.co.uk results. Nothing is run in your browser, and no bets are suggested.

How have these season probabilities scored historically?

The repo scores its title, top-four and relegation probabilities against ten completed seasons (2016-17 to 2025-26), at four points in each season. Lower Brier score is better. The "drift" variant is the uncertainty treatment used in the projections above.

The market benchmark

Match forecasts are scored with the ranked probability score (RPS, lower is better) over a rolling-origin walk-forward test on the last ten seasons, 2016-17 to 2025-26. The benchmark is the bookmakers' average closing line with the margin removed (Shin method), available for 2,660 of the 3,800 matches. The README's stated goal is parity with that line, not beating it.

Every arm's gap to the market

RPS minus the market's, on the 2,660 covered matches, with 95% intervals. Zero would be parity; every arm is on the wrong side of it.

The arms, with the acceptance rule applied

Every candidate is a change to the baseline, judged on a paired-bootstrap RPS difference, the market gap, a multiple-testing correction and a second, older decade. Older runs used a two-gate rule, and are marked. Filter to see how few survive.

Delta vs baseline: negative is better than plain Dixon-Coles. "Older decade P(better)" is the paired probability the arm beat the baseline on 2006-07 to 2015-16, where it was run.

What a forecast looks like

One saved output of the pl predict command for a typed fixture, as of the data date shown. Every market is read off a single full-time scoreline distribution.

How it works, and what to be careful with

acceptance rule (from config.yaml) Accept a change iff ALL FOUR: 1 paired-bootstrap RPS delta vs baseline favourable 2 delta vs the de-vigged market does not degrade 3 survives Benjamini-Hochberg across its family 4 the older decade does not contradict it

Leak-freedom is structural: a split raises an error whenever any training date is not strictly before every test date, and there is no k-fold anywhere. Every tunable lives in the config with a justification, and an arm only counts if the harness can show it does something.

Read this before quoting the numbers
  • One season, one projection. The 2025-26 comparison is a single case. A 49% title chance for the eventual runner-up is not an error by itself; see the historical scores above for the aggregate picture.
  • The 2025-26 projection is retrospective. It uses only data before 15 August 2025, but the file was produced afterwards.
  • The 2026-27 view covers five matchweeks only. The saved projection simulated the 50 fixtures known at the time, so it gives expected points to date, not a season forecast. Title and relegation columns are hidden for that reason.
  • Still behind the market. The production model is 0.0050 RPS behind the closing line (interval 0.0028 to 0.0071). The README says as much: parity is the goal, and apparent betting edges should be treated as bugs. This is not betting advice.
  • Left out: the 6,700-line decision ledger (NOTES.md), the raw match data, and any live fixtures feed.
